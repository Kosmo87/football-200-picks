Your bets only what you ticked · graded against the close, not just the result

Win rate cannot measure a bettor in a lifetime — the heaviest NFL favourites come along four times a season, and telling +2% from -2% at that price would take 193 of them. CLV can. It is what happened to your price after you took it: back a side at +200 that closes at +150 and the market moved toward you, which is information the price did not hold when you bet. It reads in weeks rather than decades, and it is the only number here that can judge a person rather than a model. Positive means you got the better of the closing price.

How the numbers are built
  1. Ratings — Elo per league, seeded from last season regressed to the mean, then updated game by game with a margin-of-victory multiplier and an autocorrelation damper. Preseason counts at reduced weight; neutral sites drop home-field.
  2. Model win% — logistic Elo expectation from the rating gap plus home-field.
  3. Implied win% — from the priced side, de-vigged against the other side's moneyline so both probabilities sum to 1.
  4. Edge — model win% minus implied win%, in percentage points.
  5. Sample — games of evidence behind the rating, including capped credit for last season, so a week-2 team with a real carried-over rating isn't treated as an unknown. This replaced a 0–100 “signal” score: 35 of its points were the claimed edge and 25 were a function of the price, both of which are already columns on the same row, so only the sample portion said anything new.
  6. Selection — gated legs only, no overlapping teams or games; prefer singles and short parlays that already clear the payout floor, then stack 3–5 shorter legs. Ranked by confidence and edge, not by juiciest payout. The gates are fixed, not adjustable: they came out of backtests, so they are findings rather than preferences, and a board whose filters you set would only ever recommend what you had just asked it to recommend.
  7. Stakes — the capper ladder by win probability (under 60% → 0.5U, under 70% → 1U, under 80% → 2U, and so on), capped by quarter-Kelly so a fairly priced favourite gets nothing however likely it looks, then snapped to the nearest half unit. Under a quarter unit is no bet. Distance from the market is handled by refusing the bet, not by shrinking it: applying it twice flattened every stake to the floor, because each surviving bet sits in the same trust band and the multiplier could only shrink them all alike.
  8. What gets hidden — two filters run before anything is shown. A tip the engine would stake under 0.3U on is dropped: that is the engine saying it barely believes it, and presenting it as “#1 Tip” reads as conviction it does not have. And both sides of a game are dropped when a quarterback has been ruled out, because the ratings are stale for that game in both directions — the same uninformed number says “back the injured team” on one side and “fade their opponent” on the other.
  9. Two lists — the same engine and the same gates, read in the two directions a bet can be good. Safest requires the whole ticket to be better than a coin flip (not each leg: four legs each cleared at 55% is a 9% parlay) and drops the payout floor to −400, since a bet paying +200 is a one-in-three shot by construction. Best priced keeps the +200 target and takes the largest gaps. A bet never appears in both. The market prices certainty accurately, so a likely winner pays little and a big payout is a longshot — there is no third list where both are true.
  10. Placed bets — ticking a bet records it server-side with the price and stake at that moment, and the hourly build settles it against the final score. A parlay is one ticket: one losing leg settles it, and a pushed leg drops out and re-prices the rest. “Your bets” is that record and nothing else — a recommendation you did not take never appears in it.
  11. CLV — each tracked pick logs its price when first surfaced and keeps updating the latest price until kickoff. Positive CLV means the opening price was longer than the close.

Educational prototype only — not betting advice. No guarantees of profit. Odds and results come from ESPN's public scoreboard feed and may be stale or wrong. Bet responsibly.
